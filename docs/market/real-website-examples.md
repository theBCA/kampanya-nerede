# Real Website And App Examples

These are the concrete market references found in the existing research HTML files. The point is not to copy them; it is to know what they already own and where `Fiyatı Ne` must be sharper.

## Turkey

| Example | What they do | What we should learn | Gap for Fiyatı Ne |
|---|---|---|---|
| Akakçe | Broad price comparison, product catalog, partial price history, "gerçek indirimler" language. | Users already understand price comparison and historical price charts. | Weak in cosmetic-specific needs, unit price, routine filters, and independent verdict language. |
| Cimri | Broad comparison and marketplace-oriented price discovery. | Generic "cheapest seller" is a known behavior. | Does not own the "is this discount honest?" decision layer. |
| Yaniyo | Amazon Turkey deal and price tracking app. | Simple live deal feed and target-price alerts are easy to understand. | Single merchant, Amazon affiliate incentive, limited product depth, no cross-seller net-price verdict. |
| PiyasApp | Link-based price tracking for Amazon, Hepsiburada, N11, MediaMarkt; paid Pro tier. | Price alarm willingness is already validated. | User must already know the product and paste the link. We should let users follow needs and saved filters. |
| Ne İndirimde | Multi-marketplace tracking, price history, alarms, Pro tier. | 30/90 day history and paid alert tiers exist in the user’s mental model. | Opportunity is stronger verdict quality, cosmetic depth, and seller-independent trust. |
| indirimRadar | Deal/fiyat fırsatı app. | Turkish users understand "radar" and deal stream naming. | Risk of becoming another generic deal list. |
| Kampania / Kartsever | Bank, payment, and card campaign aggregation. | Card and bank campaign data is useful but can clutter onboarding. | Keep card logic optional; do not make the MVP depend on user card setup. |
| kampanyanerede.com | Campaign comparison site with brand/payment campaign coverage. | The old name is occupied and "kampanya" points to a crowded category. | We should avoid campaign-list positioning and lead with real price verification. |
| DonanımHaber Sıcak Fırsatlar | Community deal discovery and hot/cold behavior. | Community voting can validate deal quality. | Full forum/community is too heavy for MVP; use lightweight good/bad price votes first. |

## Europe And US

| Example | What they prove | MVP implication |
|---|---|---|
| mydealz / Dealabs / HotUKDeals | Large deal communities can become valuable when they own community trust. | Do not start with an empty forum. Start with verdicts and lightweight voting. |
| iGraal / cashback players | Cashback/affiliate models create monetization but weaken independence. | First trust position should remain seller-independent. Monetization comes later through premium and B2B data. |
| Slickdeals | Community-curated deals can scale, but community operations are heavy. | MVP should not require moderation-heavy comments. |
| Honey | Checkout extension placement is powerful, but affiliate/link behavior created trust and policy risk. | Browser extension can come later; if built, it should be transparent and user-triggered. |

## Product Positioning Decision

`Fiyatı Ne` should not be described as:

- A campaign site
- A coupon site
- A marketplace
- A generic price comparison engine
- A forum

It should be described as:

- A real-price checker
- A discount verification layer
- A saved-search and price-alert product
- A trust product for repeat purchases

The strongest Turkish user phrase remains:

> Bu fiyata alınır mı?
